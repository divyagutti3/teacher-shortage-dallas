**Abstract:** 


## Introduction

Teacher staffing shortages are often discussed in terms of vacant positions, teacher turnover, and difficulties recruiting qualified educators. Administrative data can describe the existing teacher workforce, including employment, certification, and retention, while online job postings provide information about the positions advertised by the districts. However, these sources capture different aspects of staffing needs, and neither necessarily provides a complete picture of where schools are struggling to recruit and retain teachers.

This project will examine how these sources can be collected and combined to study teacher staffing demand across Texas independent school districts (ISDs). The analysis will focus on independent school districts serving any portion of Dallas County to determine what information is publicly available and whether the data can be compared consistently. Substitute teacher staffing will also be considered if reliable district-level data can be obtained.


**RQ: How can publicly available administrative data and online job postings be combined to measure teacher staffing demand across independent school districts serving any portion of Dallas County, Texas?**

A central challenge is that the number of advertised positions does not necessarily equal the number of teacher vacancies. A posting may cover multiple positions, remain online after a position has been filled, or reflect ongoing recruitment rather than an immediate staffing need. Similarly, workforce statistics such as teacher employment and attrition can help describe staffing conditions but do not, on their own, establish how many positions a district needs to fill at a given time.

The project will therefore examine what each source can contribute and where the sources have limitations. The main outcome will be the number of distinct teacher positions advertised by each participating ISD during a defined collection period. Where available, these data will be compared with relevant Texas Education Agency (TEA) workforce indicators, taking differences in reporting periods and definitions into account. Rather than assuming that these measures are interchangeable, the analysis will assess how consistently they describe staffing demand across districts.

This question is relevant because differences in data availability and reporting practices may affect how teacher staffing needs are identified. Developing a systematic approach to collecting and organizing these data could make comparisons across districts more transparent and help identify gaps in the information currently available to the public.

# Expected Contributions and Limitations
Expected Contribution and Limitations
The main contribution of this project will be a documented method for collecting and organizing information about teacher staffing demand from sources that were not necessarily designed to be used together. The analysis will show which indicators can be compared across ISDs, where the data provide an incomplete picture, and what additional information would be needed for a more comprehensive measure of staffing shortages.

The project will not assume that a larger number of online postings necessarily indicates a more severe shortage. Differences in district size, recruitment practices, posting duration, and the number of positions attached to each advertisement may influence the results. Likewise, workforce data covering an academic year may not align directly with job postings collected during a shorter period. These limitations will be considered when interpreting the findings.

Beginning with a small sample of Dallas-area ISDs will make it possible to evaluate the feasibility of the approach within the scope of the course. If the data collection process proves reliable, the method could later be extended to additional districts across Texas. The findings could also help identify whether substitute staffing information adds a useful dimension to existing workforce indicators.
